Part 1 · Decision memoryThe Second Wave · Decision Intelligence
The most expensive thing to change later is what one row stands for, so spend the time here. Judged against a standard set in advance is the phrase that does the work — it is what separates a decision record from a log.

what a second row on the same task buys you

Every resubmission is a rejected attempt followed by an accepted one.
The diff between the two is the criterion.

the pair is already the shape of the record

Attempt 1, rejected, reason incomplete documentation. Attempt 2, accepted.

Two rows, one task — the same shape preference training runs on.

⭐ why the pair beats the single row

A reason is only legible against the alternative it beat

Design rationale paired options with criteria in 1991; explainable AI reached the same conclusion in 2019 — explanations are contrastive. Both peer-reviewed.

one queue to try it on

Grant applications, change requests, credit exceptions, pull requests — any queue with a resubmission loop.

The pairs are already in there, and reading them needs no new collection.

And the diffs are already recorded, at depth. SharePoint has kept document version history since its first release in 2001, and git every superseded revision since 2005 — twenty-five years and twenty-one years of change, held as differences rather than summaries. Reading what changed between the version turned down and the version approved is reading the criterion that made it right.

⚠️ The rejected side carries no outcome — what the decider valued, not whether they were right. Selective labels, KDD 2017, peer-reviewed. ⚠️ Options that died in conversation are not in the record; that one is open. ⚠️ The older the archive, the thinner the trail — the recommended SharePoint setting trims about 96% of version storage over six months, vendor-published. Version tracking shipped in SharePoint Portal Server 2001; git’s first release was 7 April 2005.

what that one column buys you

A reported number turns out to be wrong.
Which decisions did it actually change?

the structure

Each judged attempt is a node. Each thing it cited is a link to an earlier one. Follow the links and you have the answer — for a bad reported number, a withdrawn policy, a model you no longer trust.

and it runs both ways

Walk forward from a faulty policy and you have every decision that leaned on it. A decision can rest on a reported number, a document, or an earlier decision — that is precedent.

⭐ where every chain ends

Follow one down and you reach an observation, a measured rate, or an assertion. A rule promoted off a measured rate can be re-run next quarter. One that was simply decided cannot.

The question the last wave could not answer, and this one can. Every chain terminates, because nothing can cite what did not yet exist.

PART 3

Safe delegation

Hand more decisions to people and to agents, inside a delegation you set, with the evidence captured and the escalation automatic.

WHY — this is where the return lands. More work handed over, at a fraction of the cost, with the record to show which paths were safe to promote and from when.

⭐ And it compounds. Every path you make deterministic stops needing a hosted model, and your judgement stays inside your boundary. A million tasks: $42, against $184 for a language model three points ahead — and open weights on your own hardware stop metering altogether.

who has to produce the measure of certainty

Probability is derived, not declared.
The rows give it to you, for free.

the count

Accepted over n, for each task type, reason class and worker kind

One query, over columns the record already holds.

the precision

Twenty decisions at ninety-five per cent carry a floor of seventy-six; a thousand at that rate carry ninety-four

Nothing improved. Only the evidence grew.

⭐ approaching certainty

Dividing a group more finely holds or raises its resolution, and the finer of two calibrated forecasters wins under every proper scoring rule

As sure as the evidence allows — and the evidence keeps arriving.

⚠️ It measures agreement with the standard you wrote, not ground truth. Thin groups, batched work and many groups watched at once each widen the interval; quote the floor, never the point. The other group is mixed by definition and is never promoted on. Refinement under proper scoring rules: DeGroot & Fienberg, peer-reviewed.
