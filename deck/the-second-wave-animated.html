What it asks for

Where a computer program makes, or substantially and directly helps make, a decision that could significantly affect someone — using their personal information — the privacy policy must describe the kinds of information used and the kinds of decisions made.

the vocabulary, in one place, before any of the detail

The words this talk leans on. Defined once, here, and not explained again.

Decision Intelligence

Treating your organisation’s decisions as data you can count, learn from and act on — the same way you already treat sales or incidents. ⚠️ The term is also a Mastercard product name, and an analyst category before that.

The first wave · the second wave

The first wave built the warehouse, the pipeline and the dashboard, and it recorded what happened. The second records the decision itself, and why. AI is not itself the second wave: AI is the fuel. It raises the volume of decisions far past anything the first wave recorded, and that is what makes recording them worth doing.

A judged attempt — one decision record

One attempt at one task, judged against a standard set in advance. The record states the task_type, the worker_kind, the attempt number, what was chosen, why (reason_code), what it rested on (basis_refs) and decide_seconds. Three attempts at one task produce three records. A model call is not a record, because nothing was judged against a standard.

A standard

The acceptance criterion the attempt will be judged against, written down before the attempt is made. A rule produces a decision; a standard evaluates one. The judgement is made against the criterion, not against hindsight.

A departure

An attempt that did not follow the standard course — which may be entirely correct. It is a property of the decision. The reason class conformed marks an attempt that did not depart.

A gate

A control point in a workflow where the action cannot complete until the reason is recorded. It is a property of the process, not of the decision. At the hospital the action is permission to depart from the protocol, and recording the reason is what grants it. Gated records have run for four decades; voluntary ones mostly stall.

Locus

Latin for place. For a departure, the locus states which part of the operation the stated cause sat in — the input · a rule · a person’s judgement · a resource · a party outside · or beyond the decider’s delegation. It is the parent of reason_class, the level that identifies who can act on it — and you never store it: it comes with the class.

reason_code

The value in which whoever decided states why the attempt went as it did. Six to eight per domain, the list written by the people doing the work, in their own words. It is not shared outside the organisation.

reason_class

The shared parent of reason_code. Many local codes map to one class; there are about eleven classes and they are identical across organisations. This is the only part that travels between organisations.

basis_refs

The identifiers of the records that were in front of whoever decided. Keys, not copies — the source text stays where it is, under the access controls it already has. Each identifier is a link from this record back to an earlier one, and it records what was cited, not what caused the decision.

worker_kind

Who or what made the decision. Four values: person · agent, software that chose its own next step · deterministic_code, a rule fired with no model involved · person_with_agent.

task_type · data_class · decide_seconds

The kind of work the attempt belonged to · the sensitivity class of the data used · the elapsed time to decide, in seconds.

Blue are column names you would type. Green are ideas. The three levels run reason_code → reason_class → locus — local, shared, then who can act. You store the first two; the locus is green because it comes with the class rather than being typed. The open vocabulary at github.com/ab4data/decision-record-vocabulary uses exactly these names.

What it rested on — this is the join

Identifiers of the records actually in front of whoever decided. Keys, not copies — the row stays small and the sensitive text keeps its own access controls. This is the column the last wave skipped

And each identifier is an edge. Each judged attempt is a node, each citation a directed link, and it cannot contain a cycle, because a decision can only cite what already existed. That is not a database nicety. A decision often does rest on another decision — the schema has a relation for exactly that — and acyclicity is what guarantees the chain still terminates. Follow any of them far enough and it bottoms out, because nothing can cite what did not yet exist. Two columns and a type; traversal is a recursive join, so no graph database. You already do this for data: column lineage answers "where did this number come from?" This answers "what did this judgement rest on?"

⚠️ The one way a cycle gets in is by citing a living document instead of a fixed version of it — cite the version. An edge records what was cited, not what caused the decision. Lineage, not causality — and the share recorded as "other" is a free data-quality measure.

⭐⭐ A decision resting on another decision is not a weakness in the chain. It is what makes the chain testable. Every chain terminates, and what sits at the bottom is one of three things:

An observation or a document
A measurement, a statute, a specification. Authority from outside.

A decision with a rate behind it
A rule promoted because departures fell to near zero across a counted number of cases. Authority that was earned — and can be re-tested next quarter.

A decision with nothing behind it
Somebody decided, and the basis was never written down. Authority by assertion, inherited by everything above it.

⭐ The proportions are the finding. A rule promoted off a measured rate is reproducible — you can run the measurement again and see whether it still holds. A rule that was simply decided cannot be re-tested, because there is nothing to re-test. Nobody can currently count how much of their doctrine is which. With the record you can, and that count is the first honest answer to “why do we do it this way?”

This is the question the last wave could not answer and this one can: a reported figure, a threshold inside a policy, or a model output turns out to be wrong — which decisions did it actually change?

so how do the rules get good, and how fast?

The rules get better as the record grows. Each cycle leaves the organisation knowing something about its own work that it did not know before.

A rule written on day one is a fine place to start, and so is a blank page. What the record changes is how often the organisation gets to be less wrong than it was. Each cycle returns the same three things: the departure rate against the rule as it stands, the locus naming who can change it, and a better-informed rule for the next period. Where the record does not exist, that correction waits on an incident, an audit or a complaint, so it arrives in years — and often not at all, because nobody connects the outcome back to the rule that produced it. That knowledge is held by the organisation rather than by the people, so it stays when they move on.

so why would anybody actually fill it in? — and this is our job, not the vendor’s

Make the field mandatory and you get compliance. Give people a reason and you get data.

⚠️ Mandatory on its own is already measured, and it does not work. Cause-of-death coding is a century old, mandatory worldwide, and staffed by trained coders — and 29% of a million Norwegian deaths still went to a residual code, with the share rising over 24 years. A required field buys you a value in every row. It does not buy you a true one.

⭐ 1 · Give it back to them first

The person who fills it in is the first person to see the summary, not the last. This is the one motivator with a number behind it — showing people their own decisions back measurably improves what they do next.

⭐ 2 · Let them write the list

A list handed down is an imposition. A list the team wrote in a morning is their own vocabulary — and they already know the eight answers, because they say them to each other every day.

⭐ 3 · Buy them something at the gate

The hospital model: you may depart from the standard, provided you record why. Trade it for a waiver, a fast path, or a second signature they no longer need.

4 · Retire a field as you add one

Replace the free-text box nobody reads. Net effort of zero is an easy yes; net effort of plus one is a negotiation.

5 · Let the machine draft it

Pre-fill the class and let the person correct it. Correcting is cheaper than entering — and a correction is better data than a fresh entry.

6 · Report on work, never on people

If people think it will be used against them, the quality dies before the adoption does — they pick the safe code, every time.

⭐ Watch the “other” rate, and watch how fast the first summary comes back. A climbing “other” rate is the earliest sign that people are paying the cost and getting nothing for it. Get a summary in front of them inside the first month, while the effort still feels new.
