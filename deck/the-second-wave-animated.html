What to record about the decisions your organisation makes

The Second Wave

AI is about to make far more of your organisation's decisions. What you record about each one decides how far and how fast you can widen that, and how confidently.

Two columns on a form somebody already fills in: why the decision went that way, and what it rested on. Everything else in this talk is downstream of those.

Four words that get used as if they meant the same thing

machine learning

Fit a model to labelled history

Thousands of past cases with the answer attached, and it predicts the answer for a new one. It needs your labels, at volume, before it can start

a language model

Trained to continue text

Trained once, at great expense, on very large amounts of text. You point it at a new task by asking, not by retraining — so it needs none of your labels to begin

inference

Running a trained model to get an answer

The opposite of training. Not the statistician's sense — nothing is being estimated about a population. The model is just being used

generation

One kind of inference

The kind where the output is new content — prose, code, an image. Writing a paragraph is generation. Naming what kind of thing something is, is not

an agent  A harness that chooses its own next step — it loops, picks tools, and keeps going until it judges the job done. This is what makes the volume rise.

fine-tuning  Continuing a trained model's training on your own labelled examples, so a small model gets good at one narrow task. Remember this one.

Generation is a mode of inference, not a synonym for it — and that difference carries the rest of this talk.

Asking a model "what kind of thing is this?" is inference without generation: one short answer from a list you wrote. That is a classifier, and the next slide is about why it is the essential piece.

Analyst estimates put inference at roughly two-thirds of all AI compute in 2026, up from about a third in 2023. Training is a project; inference is an operation.

This room knows "inference" as estimating a population parameter with uncertainty, and that is not what it means here — say so explicitly or half the room is quietly confused for twenty minutes. The model is one part. The harness is what makes it do work.

A harness is the software wrapped around the model — it supplies the instructions, hands over the tools, decides what the model sees, and runs the loop until the job is done. Change nothing about the model and change the harness, and you have a different system.

★ The classifier is the piece this whole proposal rests on. Three reasons, and they are the three things any proposal like this has to satisfy.

Workable. It reads free text and returns the one value you need. That is the column we have never had.

Scalable. One word out, not a paragraph — cheap enough to run on every single item, not a sample.

Safe. The answer must come from your list, so it can be marked right or wrong — and it cannot invent a category.

And it can go and read things before it answers

retrieval

Fetch the passages that bear on this request

Search your own material, put the relevant parts in front of the model, then ask. Published in 2020 and now routine — the answer is grounded in your documents rather than the model's memory of the internet

tools

Query the database, call the service, open the file

and here is the part to notice

Everything it read is a list of identifiers

five precedents, across five sectors, and each one is a piece of the foundation

This has been built before. Five times, in five industries, and the oldest has been running since 1988.

1988 · health

A hospital system

1988 · banking

A bank

An information-based strategy, in its own annual reports: test the offer on a small group, measure, let the measurement choose the next one.

2005 · research

University research

Software teams

so what is left to do? — my reading, and the survey on the next slide backs it

Five working loops. Five different shapes. Each one stayed exactly where it was built.

The hospital's

Clinical language, tied to care protocols. A genuine decision record — and unusable by anyone not running care protocols.

The bank's

At the level of a customer group, not a judgement. It tells you an offer did better — not why one decision went that way.

The software one

Prose, in a file, in one repository. Excellent to read in two years. You cannot count them or group them.

The research one

A registry entry, in trial vocabulary, one row per study. Exactly right for trials. No use for a refund.

The government one

Structured, countable, public — and shaped entirely around one statute. Perfect for content moderation in Europe, and for nothing else.

A general store was tried. Lessons learned, on paper in 1992 and online in 1994 — and its own auditors found in 2012 that contribution and use were inconsistent.

A general standard exists. Published as a web standard in 2013 for recording what produced a piece of data. Thirteen years. In use — largely not at the level of a business decision.

So it is not the idea, not the record, and not the standard. Every one was built bespoke — and bespoke things do not travel.

the promise, again

Not fact-based decisions. Automated ones.

Systems that read a request, work out what it is, and act. The volume of judgements is about to rise sharply, because what makes them is no longer limited by how many people you can hire

and the join is still open

The agent-logging standard has not settled

The open convention for recording what a model did has stabilised for single calls. For agents — multi-step, tool-using — its own status page says the semantics remain unsettled

meanwhile, in other industries

Four billion decisions, downloadable

Every large online platform in Europe files a structured record for each content decision. Every adjusted US health claim line carries a reason code. Each one is built for a single obligation in a single process

The method, and the bit worth copying

Finding the thing and deciding about it are two separate fields.

automated_detection = Yes | No
automated_decision = fully | partially | not automated

So you can tell "a machine found it and a person judged it" from "a machine did both" — which is the difference that matters, and one boolean would have lost it.

Not one row per task — a task can be attempted three times. Not one row per model call — a call is not a judgement.

And worker_kind takes four values, not two: person · agent · deterministic code · a person working with an agent. That fourth one is most of your work within two years, and it is the one I see left out of schemas most often.

⭐ And "agent" is not one worker. Every coding assistant now ships a model picker or an automatic router — Cursor, Copilot, Gemini, Claude Code. Which model answered is a choice some software made, on your behalf, and it is not in your log. Record it as a dimension while that is still cheap to do.

The standard is written when the task is created, so the measure is defined before the observation. That reverses normal practice and costs nothing.

The two columns — one the hospital had, one the software teams have

Each has been proven on its own. Having both, countable, in one place, is the part that is still unusual. A person would fill them in most times, a rule could do it some of the time, and either way the cost per decision is seconds.

reason_code

Why this one went the way it did

One value from a short list of six to eight the domain itself writes — a tick-box, not an essay, plus an optional sentence. A rate becomes a mechanism: not "acceptance fell four points" but "two thirds of the new rejections are incomplete documentation, on one task type"

basis_refs

What it rested on — this is the join

Identifiers of the records actually in front of whoever decided. Keys, not copies — the row stays small and the sensitive text keeps its own access controls. This is the column the last wave skipped

And each identifier is an edge. Each judged attempt is a node, each citation a directed link, and it cannot contain a cycle because a decision can only cite what already existed. Two columns and a type; traversal is a recursive join, so no graph database. You already do this for data: column lineage answers "where did this number come from?" This answers "what did this judgement rest on?"

An edge records what was cited, not what caused the decision. Lineage, not causality — and the share recorded as "other" is a free data-quality measure.

The basis card carries the whole argument of this option — it is the column the last wave skipped, named as such. Keep the honest limit in the footer; it is what keeps the lineage claim defensible under challenge.

question one — what shape is a real decision?

Fifty years of research says managers decide fast, by pattern — and switch into deliberate reasoning the moment they have to justify the call.

A sustained line of work, not one old paper

It opens with a 1972 model of organisational choice — choices, problems, solutions and people as four streams that meet by timing rather than by deliberation. That paper alone has been cited more than seven thousand times, about a hundred and thirty a year, every year since.

And the modern end of it is the useful part

Research on expert judgement finds the same pattern in the field: experienced people recognise and act, and move into slow deliberate reasoning when a decision has to be justified. Observed in fire services, ambulance, policing and the military.

⭐ Which makes the reason code do something better than describe the decision. Asking for a recorded reason is the justification demand — so the record is itself the thing that switches a fast call into a considered one.

Where this fits cleanly. Work handed out, come back, and judged against a standard set in advance — claim assessments, code reviews, triages, approvals with a threshold. And every decision an agent makes is exactly that shape.

Where the judgement stays human. The boardroom, and how a strategy actually got chosen — assembled afterwards out of things that happened. Those keep their own methods, and this record leaves them alone.

Cohen, March & Olsen, Administrative Science Quarterly 17:1–25 (1972) — 7,427 citations on Semantic Scholar at 10 October 2026 · expert-judgement and naturalistic decision-making literature. ⚠️ Much of that work is simulation and field study rather than controlled trial, and it is quoted here as a converging picture, not a single measurement.

⭐ The green band is the whole reason this slide earns its place, and it turns a bound into a mechanism: the research says people reason deliberately when they have to justify, and a reason code is a justification demand — so the record does not just capture the decision, it improves it at the moment it is made. Give the citation count out loud; a sustained line of work carries more weight in this room than a date. ⚠️ Say that much of the evidence is simulation and field observation rather than controlled trial — then give the two cards, which draw the boundary generously: agent work fits exactly, and the boardroom keeps its own methods.
